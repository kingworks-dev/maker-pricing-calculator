Frequently asked

The short answers handmade sellers look up most.

How do I price handmade items?

Add materials, labor (hours times your hourly rate) and overhead or packaging per item to get your cost. Multiply the cost by one plus your markup to get the retail price. On $9 of materials, 1.5 hours at $20 and $4 of packaging, cost is $43.00 and a 150% markup gives a $107.50 retail price.

What is the difference between markup and margin?

Markup is profit as a percentage of cost. Margin is profit as a percentage of the selling price. A 150% markup on a $43.00 cost gives $64.50 of profit, which is a 60.0% margin on the $107.50 price.

How much should I charge for wholesale?

The common convention is half of retail. At a $107.50 retail price, wholesale is $53.75, which leaves $10.75 profit per item on a $43.00 cost. At a 100% markup the same math leaves zero profit at wholesale, so set retail high enough that half of it still clears your cost.

Should I pay myself an hourly rate in my price?

Yes. Leaving labor out is the most common reason a craft business looks busy and earns nothing. Pick the hourly rate you would accept from an employer for the same skill, and let the calculator carry it into every price.
